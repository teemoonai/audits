# teemoonai/teemoon-android @ v1.1.0-3918 (ee2c8b3d6673e87bea6310de598b01025933e72f) — 1.1.0 (3918)

## verdict: QUALIFIED-PASS — no way was found for the app to pass a key or a message to anyone but the chosen provider without the user's action; two medium-severity issues were found after release (the app verifies one of several near.ai enclaves that share a key; and when Intel's service cannot be reached, the app's own hardware check can be passed without real hardware, behind a question the user must accept); one low-severity issue is open and accepted for 1.1.0; a real phone and a live-provider run of the signed release app were not part of the review

## Summary

**What was checked.** The source code of teemoon for Android 1.1.0 (3918), as published under the tag `v1.1.0-3918`. Everything that changed since 1.0.6 was read. The whole app was searched for every place where an API key or a message could be written to storage, a log or the clipboard, or sent over the network. The app's tests were run, the app was run with real providers on a test build of the final source code, and the release app was installed over 1.0.6. All of this was on an Android emulator.

**What was found.** No way was found for the app to pass your API keys or your messages to anyone except the provider you chose, unless you take an action such as sending, sharing or copying. Two medium-severity issues were found after release and are described first below. One low-severity issue is open and was accepted for this release. There are eleven minor notes. Four issues recorded against 1.0.6 are fixed.

**What was not checked.** No physical phone was used. The runs with real providers used a test build of the final source code, not the signed release app, which the test tools cannot drive. The inside of the bundled image-decoding library was not read. The Google Play edition contains one Google library (the rating prompt) that the edition reviewed here does not, and that library was not reviewed.

### Is my data safe?

This review found no path by which the app itself gives your keys or your messages to anyone but the provider you choose. It is teemoon's own AI-assisted review, not an audit by an outside firm, and it cannot tell you what a provider does with a message after it arrives; only near.ai models that run in attested secure enclaves are end-to-end encrypted, and for those the app checks one of the enclaves before it sends (see the first finding).

## What was found, in plain terms

### The one to know about: the app checks one near.ai enclave, but several hold the key (medium severity, found after release)

- **What can happen.** Before it sends to a near.ai model, the app checks proof of the hardware and code that will read your message. near.ai runs each model on more than one secure enclave, and they share one encryption key. The app checks the first enclave near.ai lists. Your message is sealed to the shared key, so the other enclaves can open it too, and the app has not checked them.
- **When.** Every message to an end-to-end encrypted near.ai model. On 2026-11-18, three enclaves with different configurations held the key for glm-5.3-flash, and two held each DeepSeek model's key.
- **Does it affect you?** Only if you use near.ai models. Your message still goes only to near.ai and is still sealed. Every key holder seen was a genuine secure enclave, and their configurations are public and covered by teemoon's reviews of near.ai. What is weaker than the app's screen says: "verified" describes one enclave, not every enclave that can read the message, and the sentence that the key lives inside the model's sealed chip is not accurate.
- **Planned fix.** Planned for version 1.1.1: check every enclave near.ai lists, and correct the wording. That will not cover an enclave near.ai does not list. Which enclaves receive the key is decided by near.ai's key service, which the app cannot check today.

### When Intel's service cannot be reached, the app's own hardware check can be passed without real hardware (medium severity, found after release)

- **What can happen.** Before it sends to a near.ai model, the app checks the hardware proof in two ways: a check on the phone itself, and a fuller check that needs data from Intel's servers. If Intel's servers cannot be reached, the app asks "still checking near.ai's hardware" and lets you send anyway. In that moment only the check on the phone stands behind the encryption key, and that check has a gap: it does not confirm that the key which signed the proof belongs to Intel's hardware. Someone who can answer in near.ai's place could present a made-up proof with their own encryption key, and the check on the phone would accept it. If you then choose to send, that message is sealed to their key and they can read it.
- **When.** Only when three things hold at once: someone can answer as near.ai under a certificate your phone accepts (near.ai's own gateway, or a network that inspects encrypted traffic with a certificate installed on the phone); Intel's servers cannot be reached from your phone at that moment; and you tap to send anyway when the app asks. If Intel's servers answer, the made-up proof is rejected and the send is blocked. Nothing is sent without the question.
- **Does it affect you?** Only if you use near.ai models, and only if you send anyway when the app says it is still checking near.ai's hardware. If you see that question, wait and try again rather than sending something sensitive. This has not been seen to happen. The question's wording is weaker than it should be: it speaks of a check that has not finished, when the check could not be done.
- **Planned fix.** Planned for version 1.1.1 (the maintainer, 2026-11-20): the check on the phone confirms that the signing key belongs to Intel's own quoting enclave, so a made-up proof is rejected whether or not Intel's servers answer.

### A near.ai model can stop being end-to-end encrypted without an alert (low severity, accepted for 1.1.0)

- **What can happen.** A near.ai model that you have been using end-to-end encrypted (sealed so only the model can read it) can change to "proxied". After that, your messages to that model still go to near.ai, the provider you chose, over an ordinary encrypted connection. They are no longer sealed to the model, so the operator (near.ai) could read them. The app shows the new label "proxied" next to the model. It does not show an alert.
- **When.** Only if near.ai changes how it lists that model, or if someone able to impersonate near.ai's server sends the app a false list. This has not been seen to happen.
- **Does it affect you?** Only if you use near.ai models and rely on end-to-end encryption. If you do, look at the label next to the model before you send something sensitive. A message that is being sent at the moment of the change is dropped, not sent unsealed. Models on your phone, on your own computer and at other providers are not affected.
- **Planned fix.** Planned for version 1.1.1. The proposed change: the app asks once before the first unsealed message to a model you have used sealed. The maintainer accepted the issue for 1.1.0 on 2026-11-15.

### Traced — 5. Images and the isolated decoder (H4)
Paths under `img/` are `app/src/main/kotlin/ai/teemoon/app/imaging/`.

**Shape.** Every image that enters the app — photo picker, camera, paste, share-in, import — is bytes handed to a bound service in a separate `isolatedProcess` (`AndroidManifest.xml:136-140`): no permissions, no network, no access to the app's files. ### MEDIUM (deployed: ON; addendum 2026-11-19, mechanism present at 1.0.6) — gate: only the first listed model enclave is verified, and every enclave near.ai's key service admits under the app id holds the same key

Added after publication. Reported by the iOS developer session on 2026-11-18 and confirmed by the orchestrator the same day.

- **Where.** `confidential/src/main/kotlin/ai/teemoon/confidential/AttestationService.kt:144` (`first` = `allAttestations?.firstOrNull() ?: modelAttestations?.firstOrNull()`) and `:163` (the info legs take the first element of each list). Everything the record carries (quote, compose hash, OS image hash, provenance, the send gate's predicates) describes that one element.
- **Observed.** The orchestrator fetched `cloud-api.near.ai/v1/attestation/report` without a key and with its own nonce on 2026-11-18. For `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash` the gateway lists three model attestations: one `app_id` (`2c0a0c96…`), three instance ids, three compose hashes (`c900dc73…`, `e8416a02…`, `a7de0bba…`), and the same signing public key in all three, for ed25519 and for ecdsa. For the two DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 models it lists two each (compose `bd0a5446…` and `08f91377…`), same `app_id`, one key per model. The direct model host returns one enclave per call, and which one varied between calls.
- **Why the key is shared.** In the dstack tree these guests run, the key service derives the application key from the app id alone; the compose hash feeds only its decision to release (`kms/src/crypto.rs`, `derive_k256_key`; read by the orchestrator, and on the [guest OS page](/os/sha256-3bb7d262416f47db06855d7b7cf7d380538747206014b3c0adcddfef9b7c4ef2.md)). So the key belongs to the app id, not to a measurement, and a quote from one enclave shows that enclave holds the key, not that it alone does.
- **Effect.** A sealed message can be opened by enclaves the app did not verify. The reply-signature check cannot tell them apart: they sign with the same key. A recipe-hash mismatch or an unpublished image on an unverified enclave is not seen, so the block that this release's gate fix adds applies to the enclave checked only. The message still goes only to near.ai, sealed; every holder observed is a TDX enclave with a valid quote for the nonce, and their measured configurations have pages in this repository.
- **What the user is told.** The trust screen says the message is "sealed so only" the model can open it and that the key "lives inside" the model's sealed chip. Neither sentence is accurate as written.
- **Deployed state.** ON for every near.ai model served by more than one enclave. The same reading is at 1.0.6 (`git show 63cef559:confidential/src/main/kotlin/ai/teemoon/confidential/AttestationService.kt`, `:127-139`); the 1.0.6 page carries the same addendum.
- **Fix and what it cannot close.** Planned for 1.1.1 (proposed by the developers; the maintainer confirmed the plan on 2026-11-19): verify every listed attestation and require the checks to pass for all. That covers listed enclaves only. An enclave the gateway does not list still holds the key if the key service admitted it under the app id; the admission policy (which compose hashes, who can add one, whether a change is visible) is not visible to the app and has not been examined here. Each enclave's attested event log does record a `key-provider` identity and an `mr-kms` value, the same in all five enclaves seen; the app reads neither.

### MEDIUM (deployed: ARMED; addendum 2026-11-20, mechanism present at 1.1.0 and not looked for in earlier releases) — gate: the on-device quote check does not tie the quote's signing key to Intel's certificate chain, and when Intel's collateral cannot be fetched the send-anyway ask rests on that check alone

Added after publication, by the orchestrator. Found during the review of the 1.1.1 candidate on 2026-11-19 (first read by the two independent reviewers, then confirmed by a constructed quote).

- **Where.** `confidential/src/main/kotlin/ai/teemoon/confidential/TdxQuote.kt:138-144` (`TdxQuoteVerifier.verify`) and `TdxVerificationResult.kt:40` (`isVerified` is `signatureValid && certChainValid`); the parser at `TdxQuote.kt:97-105` steps over the Quoting Enclave report by its fixed size. `DcapService.kt:73-84` (`collateralUnavailable`, `couldntCheck`: an I/O error, a 401/403/408/429 or 5xx, or an unreadable answer from Intel is `Unchecked`, not a failure). `ConfidentialSession.kt:1380` (`couldntCheckHardware`), `:1173` (degraded), `:1388` (`softButSealed`), `:591`.
- **Mechanism.** The on-device verifier accepts a quote when (1) the signature over the quote's header and report body verifies under the attestation key carried in the quote itself, and (2) the certificate chain carried in the quote validates to the pinned Intel root. Nothing ties (1) to (2): the Quoting Enclave's report, its signature under the PCK certificate and its commitment to the attestation key are skipped by the parser and never checked. A report body written by anyone, signed with any fresh P-256 key, beside a certificate chain copied from any genuine quote, reads as verified. The fuller check (dcap-qvl with Intel's collateral) does make that binding and rejects such a quote.
- **Why it reaches the gate.** An `Unchecked` result is a soft state: the session is degraded (`ConfidentialSession.kt:1172-1173`) and the send gate's answer is a confirm with the "still checking" ask (`softButSealed`, `:1388`; `conformance/vendor/sendgate.json:12`, vector `outage/intel-pcs`: dcap unchecked, policy confirm, alert stillChecking). On confirm the turn is sealed to the model key in the record. In that state the on-device verifier is the only hardware evidence for that key.
- **What an attacker needs, all three.** (a) To answer the attestation requests in near.ai's place under a certificate the device accepts for near.ai's host: near.ai's gateway or its host, or a TLS-inspecting network whose root the device trusts. (b) Intel's collateral unreachable from the device at that moment: the same network can block it, and a real outage does it too. A 400, 404 or 422 on the two requests built from the quote's own fields is treated as evidence and blocks, so the forged quote cannot bring this state about by itself. (c) The user confirming the ask. Then that turn is sealed to a key the attacker chose.
- **What does not happen.** No send without the ask. While the fuller check is pending the send is held. With Intel reachable the forged quote is a hard failure and the send is blocked. The other legs do not catch it in the armed state: the gateway's quote is checked by the same on-device verifier, a copied compose, event log and `mr_config_id` replay cleanly because the forger writes the measured body, and absent NVIDIA evidence is a soft state.
- **Evidence and its limits.** The mechanism was read at this commit. A constructed entry (report data rewritten to a chosen key and the request nonce, header and body signed with a fresh key, the genuine chain left in place) was run by the orchestrator on 2026-11-19 against the Android 1.1.1 candidate `f2f859bc884b4fea9983bb6eaecb0027e3111978`, whose verifier file is byte-identical to the 1.1.0 one: `signatureValid=true certChainValid=true isVerified=true`, and the candidate's listed-enclave check returned clean. The iOS developers reproduced it on the iOS verifier the same day, and report a failing test for the send-anyway path with Intel unreachable; that test is theirs and was not run by the orchestrator. Not run against a 1.1.0 build. Releases before 1.1.0 were not checked for this; the verifier is older than 1.1.0.
- **What the user is told.** The ask says near.ai's hardware is still being checked. In this state the fuller check did not run and nothing is in flight.
- **Deployed state.** ARMED for every sealed near.ai chat: it needs (a), (b) and (c) together.
- **Severity.** MEDIUM, proposed by the orchestrator and to be rated by the independent reviewer on the 1.1.1 page. Not higher: it needs a position on near.ai's host name, an unreachable Intel service and the user's confirmation, and a network that already inspects TLS reads every other provider's traffic without any of this. Not lower: end-to-end encryption to an attested enclave is the one promise meant to hold against exactly that network and against the gateway, and here it does not.
- **Fix.** Planned for 1.1.1 (the maintainer decided on 2026-11-20 that it is fixed at the root): the on-device verifier checks the Quoting Enclave report's signature under the PCK certificate, its commitment to the attestation key, and that the report is Intel's TD Quoting Enclave's. TCB status and revocation remain with the fuller check, so with Intel unreachable the app still asks.

### Residuals — by design, stated not hidden

Controls outside the app are listed here as residuals, never as mitigations.

- **One decoder process serves successive images** (5): a decoder compromised by one image could alter a later
  one. The main-process parse of its reply is now a finding (INFO above).
- **History images go to the model chosen now.** Switching to another vision provider re-sends the thread's
  earlier images to that provider with the next turn, as text history always has (5).
- **The near.ai tier list cannot upgrade.** A hostile list cannot make a vendor-named model read as sealed
  (2, H1e). It can downgrade a sealed model to proxied, after which sends go as plain TLS with no ask: that
  is the LOW finding above, not a residual.
- **After a soft ask is accepted, the peer is whatever the unverified record names.** The message is still
  sealed to that record's key (2, 3).
- **Ciphertext length shows attachment size** to the network and the provider's gateway (3).
- **Image files are unlinked, not overwritten**; database rows are scrubbed but WAL pages persist until
  checkpoint (7). Protection is the app sandbox and file-based encryption.
- **Files left on process death:** a camera capture in progress and the import staging copy; both in private
  storage, swept on the next launch or arrival (6, 7).
- **A copied image is an ordinary clipboard entry**: not marked sensitive, not cleared, readable through the
  FileProvider grant by whatever the user pastes into (10). A copied error card includes chat text (sweep).
- **Links and sources, including those in imported chats, open any scheme on tap** through the system (8).
- **The Play bundle is not the audited APK.** It adds Google's in-app review library; this page covers the
  GitHub APK's source set (Build configuration).
- **The system log** is readable by the shell user and by system-privileged apps; the lines the app writes are
  content-free as read (9).
- **Backup and device transfer** rest on `allowBackup=false` and the extraction rules (7); platform behaviour
  not measured here.
- Carried from 1.0.6 without re-reading: the privacy-page wording, `reasoning_effort` and `max_tokens` beside
  the sealed messages, the self-hosted first-send probe, the 400 effort retry, the shared connection pool.
